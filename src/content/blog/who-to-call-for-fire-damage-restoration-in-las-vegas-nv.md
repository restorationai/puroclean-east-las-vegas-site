## When Is It a Restoration Call vs. Something Else?

Fire damage restoration covers everything from the moment the fire is out to the moment your home looks and smells like it did before. That includes soot removal, smoke odor treatment, structural drying from suppression water, contents cleaning, and pack-out of salvageable belongings.

Here is a quick triage guide:

- **Active fire or structural collapse risk**, 911, stay out.
- **Gas smell after the fire**, call NV Energy (1-800-962-5325) before anyone enters.
- **Electrical hazard**, utility shutoff first, then restoration crew.
- **Source of water still flowing** (a burst pipe from suppression), a plumber to stop the flow, then the restoration crew to dry and clean.
- **Soot, smoke odor, charred materials, water from suppression**, that is a restoration call, not a plumber and not a contractor.

For homes near Summerlin or Centennial Hills where newer construction uses engineered lumber, smoke penetration into wall cavities can be significant even in a contained kitchen fire. IICRC-certified technicians use thermal imaging and air sampling to find hidden smoke migration, a general contractor will not.

## Smoke Damage Without a Visible Fire: Still Call a Restoration Company

Not every fire damage call involves charred walls. A contained stovetop fire, a smoldering electrical issue, or a neighbor's fire that pushed smoke into your unit through shared HVAC can leave soot and odor that look minor but penetrate deeply into porous materials.

Soot particles are acidic. On metal surfaces, etching begins within hours. On fabric and upholstery, odor compounds bond to fibers within days. DIY cleaning with household products often drives soot deeper into porous surfaces rather than removing it.
