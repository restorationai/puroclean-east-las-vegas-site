A flooded cabin or outbuilding in this stretch of Eldorado Canyon doesn't behave like a flooded house in the suburbs: the water often arrives fast, from a wash or a failed well line, and there's no city sewer or municipal water main to shut down the source while you wait. Water damage restoration here has to account for that isolation from the first phone call.

## Why Nelson Properties See Water Damage Issues

Nelson sits in a narrow desert canyon where summer monsoon storms can send a wash from dry to running in under an hour. Flat roofs and older mining-era wood and masonry structures common in this part of Clark County weren't built with modern drainage in mind, so a heavy July or August storm often means water finding its way under a slab or through a roof seam that's been fine for years. Add in the fact that most properties here run on private wells and septic systems rather than municipal lines, and a cracked supply pipe or a failed pressure tank can dump hundreds of gallons into a crawlspace long before anyone notices, since there's no city water bill spike to flag it early.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Nelson

We start the same way anywhere: stop the source, extract standing water, and map moisture with thermal imaging and penetrating meters so we're not guessing where the water traveled. In Nelson, that mapping step matters more than usual, because older board-and-batten or adobe-style walls absorb and release moisture differently than standard drywall, often holding water deeper into the wall cavity than the surface readings suggest. Drying is set up and monitored per the IICRC S500 structural drying standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed to account for the dry desert air outside working against trapped moisture inside. Because many Nelson properties are seasonal or part-time residences, we also check for compounding issues like dust intrusion mixing with standing water, which can slow drying and affect indoor air quality if it's not addressed during extraction.
