## Why Nellis AFB Properties See Water Damage Issues

The Las Vegas Valley sits on a layer of caliche, a hard, calcium-cemented soil that does not drain the way topsoil does elsewhere. When a sprinkler line breaks, a water heater fails, or monsoon rain overwhelms a roof drain near Nellis AFB, water tends to pool against foundations instead of soaking away, which pushes moisture up through slab cracks and expansion joints. Add in evaporative coolers, which are still common on older homes and outbuildings in this part of the valley, and you have a second common source: condensate lines and roof-mounted units that leak slowly into ceiling cavities for weeks before a stain finally shows. Flat and low-slope roofing, typical on both residential and support structures in the area, also struggles during the short but intense monsoon bursts that hit from July through September, when an inch of rain in under an hour can back up drains that were never sized for it.
