**Burst pipe or frozen line at your Mount Charleston cabin?** Call PuroClean of East Las Vegas now for emergency service. At this elevation, a hard freeze doesn't just slow water flow, it can split copper and PEX overnight, and a cabin left unoccupied for even a few days can end up with standing water under the floors by the time anyone notices.

Mount Charleston sits high enough above the valley floor that winter temperatures routinely stay below freezing for days at a stretch, something almost no other property we serve in the Las Vegas area has to deal with. Many homes up here are seasonal or weekend cabins, which means a frozen supply line can go undetected far longer than it would in a year-round residence. Add in older cabin construction with exposed crawl spaces, well systems, and plumbing runs along uninsulated exterior walls, and you get a pattern we see almost exclusively in this part of Clark County: pipes that freeze solid, then rupture during a thaw cycle and release water into framing that's already cold and slow to dry.

## Why Mount Charleston Properties See Burst & Frozen Pipe Issues

The combination of elevation, snowpack, and part-time occupancy is the real driver here. A cabin that sits closed up through a cold snap loses whatever residual heat kept pipes from freezing in the first place, and once the water inside a line turns to ice, expansion does the rest. We also see failures at hose bibs, well pump housings, and shutoff valves in unheated outbuildings, locations a typical Las Vegas valley home simply doesn't have. Because many properties here rely on private wells rather than municipal water, a burst line can also mean a pressure tank or pump that's been running dry, which compounds the damage once the leak is found.

## Our Burst & Frozen Pipes Process in Mount Charleston

The first call is about stopping the source, locating the main shutoff (not always obvious in a cabin that changes hands seasonally) and getting water off at the pump if the break is on the supply side. From there we extract standing water, check subfloor and crawl space insulation for saturation, and set up drying equipment sized for the structure, not just the room. Because many cabins have log, T&G paneling, or other natural wood interiors instead of standard drywall, drying follows the IICRC S500 standard but we adjust the plan, wood releases moisture much more slowly than drywall and can look dry at the surface while still holding water underneath. We also check for ice still sitting in adjacent pipe runs before we close out a job, since a second freeze on an already-weakened line is a common callback cause in mountain properties.

## Reaching Mount Charleston from Las Vegas

## Mount Charleston Insurance & HOA Coordination

Because Mount Charleston is unincorporated Clark County, permitting and inspection for any structural repair work goes through the county rather than a city building department, which matters if framing or subfloor needs to be opened up and replaced after a pipe failure. On the insurance side, carriers often ask more questions about occupancy and maintenance history for seasonal cabins than they do for full-time residences, so we document the source of the break, the duration of exposure if known, and the affected materials clearly to support the claim.

## Local note

One thing most visitors don't realize: a surprising number of Mount Charleston cabins still run on galvanized or early copper plumbing installed decades ago, long before PEX became standard. That older metal is more brittle in freeze conditions and tends to split rather than just crack, which usually means a faster, more forceful leak once the thaw hits, and a narrower window before water reaches finished flooring or log walls.
