**Burst pipe in your Las Vegas home right now?** Call now for emergency service before a few gallons a minute turns into saturated drywall, swollen baseboards, and water tracking into rooms you haven't even noticed yet.

A pipe doesn't have to fully rupture to cause real damage. A hairline split in a copper supply line, a cracked PEX fitting behind a washing machine, or a failed slab joint under tile can push hundreds of gallons into wall cavities and subfloor before anyone hears dripping. Water finds the path of least resistance, which in most Las Vegas homes means it travels along top plates, down inside wall cavities, and pools where you least expect it, often two rooms away from the actual break.

## What burst pipe cleanup and repair actually involves

This work sits at the intersection of plumbing and structural drying. The water source has to be addressed (or already shut off) before any cleanup starts, because pumping out standing water while a line is still pressurized is pointless. From there, the job is about tracking where the water traveled, not just where it's visible. That means pulling baseboards, checking behind cabinet toe-kicks, and probing drywall with a moisture meter instead of relying on what looks wet at a glance. Equipment typically includes submersible pumps for standing water, air movers positioned to push air through wall cavities and under flooring, and commercial dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage. Depending on how long the pipe was leaking before discovery, drying can run anywhere from two to five days, with moisture readings checked daily until wood framing and subfloor return to a dry standard.

## Our process

1. **Confirm the source is isolated.** Before anything else, we verify the water supply to the affected line is shut off, coordinating with a plumber if the break itself still needs repair.
2. **Extract standing water.** Pumps and extraction wands remove bulk water from floors, carpet, and any low points before drying equipment goes in.
3. **Map the spread.** Moisture meters and thermal imaging trace water migration inside walls, under flooring, and into adjoining rooms so nothing gets missed and closed back up wet.
4. **Set containment and drying equipment.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed based on the moisture map, not a generic room count, with daily monitoring until readings stabilize.
5. **Document and rebuild.** Affected drywall, baseboard, flooring, or cabinetry that can't be dried in place gets removed and documented for repair, with a written scope covering what's being replaced and why.

## What separates a good burst pipe response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating this as a surface cleanup instead of a structural drying job. Drying only the visible wet spot while leaving a damp wall cavity behind it is how mold shows up three weeks later in a space nobody reopened. Another frequent miss is pulling drying equipment too early because the carpet feels dry to the touch, even though the subfloor or sill plate underneath is still holding moisture. Insurance adjusters reviewing a claim will look for daily moisture logs, photos of the affected areas before demolition, and documentation showing drying equipment ran until readings hit an acceptable range, not just until a tech's schedule cleared up. Skipping that documentation is the single biggest reason claims get delayed or disputed.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Las Vegas doesn't deal with freeze-thaw cycles the way colder climates do, but it has its own pipe failure patterns. Many homes built from the 1970s through the 1990s still have original galvanized or early-generation PEX lines that are now reaching the age where fittings fail without warning. Slab-on-grade construction, common across the valley, means a slab leak often shows up as a warm or damp spot on tile or carpet long before anyone sees water, and the repair almost always involves cutting into the slab itself. Rare hard freezes do happen most winters, and homes with exposed exterior plumbing or poorly insulated garages see a spike in burst pipe calls during those cold snaps.
