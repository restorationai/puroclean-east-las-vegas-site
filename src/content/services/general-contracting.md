**Water damage dried but the drywall never got replaced. Fire damage stopped spreading but the kitchen is still down to studs. A slab leak got repaired months ago and the flooring still doesn't match.** Renovations, remodels, and general contracting in Las Vegas cover both of these situations: homeowners starting a kitchen or bathroom remodel from scratch, and property owners who went through a water, fire, or storm loss and now need the structure put back together correctly, not just patched.

## What renovations, remodels and general contracting actually involves

This work ranges from a single-room post-damage rebuild (drywall, flooring, paint, trim) to a full kitchen or bathroom remodel involving cabinetry, plumbing rough-in, electrical, and tile work. For a loss-driven rebuild, the scope is usually dictated by what was removed during mitigation: cut drywall gets replaced to match existing texture, flooring that was pulled gets re-laid, cabinets damaged by water or smoke get replaced or refinished. For a planned remodel, the timeline depends on permitting and material lead times more than labor. A single bathroom gut-and-rebuild typically runs two to four weeks once permits clear. A kitchen remodel with layout changes, especially anything touching plumbing or load-bearing walls, usually runs four to eight weeks. Older Las Vegas homes, particularly those built before the 1990s with original cast iron or galvanized plumbing, often surface secondary repair needs once walls are opened.

## Our process

1. **Scope of work.** A written scope documents what's being rebuilt, the materials specified, and how it ties to the insurance estimate if this is a loss-related job. Homeowners doing a discretionary remodel get the same documented scope, so there's no ambiguity about what's included.
2. **Permits.** Las Vegas and Clark County both require permits for structural work, electrical, plumbing, and most drywall-to-stud replacement. We pull the applicable permits before framing starts, not after.
3. **Framing and rough-in.** Structural repairs, wall framing, electrical, and plumbing rough-in happen before anything gets closed up. This is also when an inspector typically signs off before drywall goes back.
4. **Finishing.** Drywall, texture matching, paint, flooring, cabinetry, and trim. Texture and paint matching against existing, undamaged areas is where a lot of rebuild quality gets judged.
5. **Final walkthrough.** Punch list items get addressed and, for insurance-related rebuilds, documentation is assembled to close out the claim.

## What separates a good rebuild from a bad one

The most common failure point is mismatched texture and paint sheen between repaired and existing drywall, which is obvious under certain lighting even when the repair itself is structurally sound. Another is skipping permits on work that technically requires them, which creates problems later at resale or refinancing when the lack of permit history surfaces. On post-damage rebuilds specifically, a rushed contractor will close up walls before confirming the framing and subfloor are fully dry, which can trap moisture behind new drywall. Adjusters reviewing a rebuild estimate are generally looking for whether the scope matches what was actually removed during mitigation, whether like-for-like materials were used where the policy calls for it, and whether the work is documented with photos at each stage, not just before and after.

## What does renovations, remodels and general contracting cost?

Costs vary widely based on square footage, finish level, and whether the project is a cosmetic remodel or a structural rebuild tied to a prior loss. Every scope is different, which is why we provide a written estimate before any work begins rather than a verbal ballpark. For loss-related rebuilds, homeowners insurance typically covers the cost to restore the home to its pre-loss condition under the dwelling coverage portion of the policy, though upgrades beyond that baseline are usually an out-of-pocket difference.

| Scenario | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Single room repaint and flooring (post-damage) | $2,500 - $6,000 |
| Drywall and trim rebuild, one room | $4,000 - $10,000 |
| Bathroom remodel, standard finishes | $8,000 - $18,000 |
| Kitchen remodel, mid-range finishes | $20,000 - $45,000 |
| Structural rebuild after major water or fire loss | $25,000 - $75,000+ |

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Las Vegas summers push interior temperatures high enough that paint, texture, and adhesive cure times need to be managed carefully, especially in garages or rooms without active HVAC during construction. Many homes in the valley sit on slab foundations, so any rebuild involving plumbing relocation means cutting and re-pouring concrete, which adds time most homeowners don't anticipate. Stucco exteriors common throughout the valley also require a specific color and texture match if exterior repairs are part of the scope, since mismatched stucco patches are highly visible in direct desert sun.
