**Smoke damage emergency in Las Vegas?** Call now for emergency service. Smoke residue sets into drywall, cabinetry, and HVAC ductwork within hours of a fire going out, and the longer it sits, the harder it is to remove without replacing the material underneath it.

Even a small kitchen fire or a wildfire smoke event that never touched your property with flame can leave behind an acidic residue that etches metal, yellows paint, and carries an odor that air fresheners won't mask. The smell isn't sitting on the surface, it's inside the pores of plaster, grout lines, and fabric fibers. That's what separates smoke damage restoration from a deep cleaning job: the goal isn't to make a room smell better for a week, it's to neutralize the residue at a molecular level so the odor doesn't come back when summer heat drives Las Vegas temperatures into triple digits and reactivates whatever was left behind.

## What smoke damage restoration actually involves

Smoke damage restoration starts with identifying what kind of soot you're dealing with, because dry protein soot from a stovetop fire behaves nothing like the wet, sticky synthetic soot left by a burning mattress or an electrical fire involving plastics. Protein soot is often nearly invisible but carries an intense odor; synthetic soot is heavier, smears when touched, and penetrates porous surfaces fast. The crew's approach, equipment, and cleaning agents change depending on which one they're facing, and misreading it is one of the most common reasons a smoke odor comes back weeks after a first cleaning attempt.

The work itself covers hard surfaces, soft contents, and air systems separately. Walls, ceilings, and trim get chemical sponging or wet cleaning depending on the soot type. Upholstery, drapes, and clothing often go through ultrasonic cleaning or ozone treatment off-site. HVAC ductwork gets inspected and cleaned because a system that recirculated smoke during the fire will keep redistributing odor through the house every time it kicks on. A straightforward single-room fire can be resolved in a few days; a whole-house fire with contents pack-out and duct cleaning typically runs one to two weeks before everything is back in place.

## Our process

1. **Soot characterization.** Before any cleaning begins, the residue is identified as protein-based, synthetic, fuel oil, or a wildfire particulate mix, since each requires a different cleaning agent and method.
2. **Containment and pre-cleaning.** Affected rooms are sealed off to stop residue from migrating to unaffected areas, and loose surface soot is removed with dry sponges before any liquid cleaner touches the surface, otherwise the soot just smears deeper in.
3. **Thermal fogging and deodorization.** Heated deodorizing fog is used to penetrate the same pores and cracks the smoke originally penetrated, neutralizing odor at the source rather than masking it.
4. **Ozone or hydroxyl treatment.** For stubborn odor in structural materials and HVAC systems, ozone or hydroxyl generators run in sealed, unoccupied spaces to break down odor-causing compounds in the air and on surfaces.
5. **Contents pack-out and cleaning.** Salvageable furniture, textiles, and personal items are inventoried, packed out, cleaned off-site, and returned once the structure clears a final odor check.

## What separates a good smoke damage response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating smoke damage like a painting job: repaint over the stain and call it done. Paint doesn't seal in an odor that's embedded in the substrate behind it. A few weeks later, usually when the air conditioning cycles on a hot afternoon, the smell returns because the source was never actually removed.

Another frequent miss is skipping the HVAC system entirely. If the fire happened anywhere near a return vent, soot has almost certainly traveled through the ductwork, and running the system without cleaning it first just redistributes residue through every room on that line. Insurance adjusters evaluating a fire claim will typically check whether ductwork was addressed, whether soot type was documented with photos before cleaning started, and whether a moisture or odor reading was taken afterward to confirm the job actually resolved rather than just covered the issue. Documentation at each stage, before-and-after readings, and a written scope matter as much to the claim outcome as the cleaning itself.
