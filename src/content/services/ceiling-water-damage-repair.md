**Water coming through your ceiling?** A brown ring spreading across drywall, a bubble forming overhead, or an actual drip landing on the carpet means water has already saturated the material above it, and the longer it sits, the more likely that ceiling is to sag, crack, or fail outright. Call now for emergency service before the leak source gets worse or the ceiling gives way under its own weight.

## What ceiling water damage repair actually involves

A leaking ceiling is rarely just a ceiling problem. Water that stains drywall or plaster has usually traveled from somewhere else, a roof penetration, a second-floor bathroom, an HVAC condensation line, or a supply line inside a wall cavity, and it travels along joists and sheathing before it ever shows up as a stain. Ceiling water damage repair starts with finding that source, because repainting over a stain without stopping the leak just means the same spot fails again in a few months.

The repair itself depends on how far the water traveled and how long it sat. A fresh, small stain from a quick fix might only need the drywall dried in place and repainted. A sagging section, a soft spot you can push a finger into, or insulation that's visibly wet means the drywall has to come down, the cavity has to dry out completely, and the ceiling has to be rebuilt from the joists up. Timelines run from a single day for a cosmetic patch to a week or more when framing, insulation, and multiple drywall layers are involved.

## Our process

1. **Leak source identification.** Before any drywall comes down, we trace the water path, checking roofing, plumbing stacks, tub surrounds, and HVAC lines above the stain to confirm the leak is stopped or isolated.
2. **Moisture mapping.** We use moisture meters and, where needed, thermal imaging to map how far the water spread laterally across the ceiling cavity, since visible staining almost always understates the actual wet area.
3. **Controlled demolition.** Saturated drywall, insulation, and any affected framing members are removed to the point where materials read dry, not just to the edge of the visible stain.
4. **Structural drying.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are set to dry the remaining framing and surrounding materials per the IICRC S500 standard, with daily moisture readings until the cavity reads dry.
5. **Rebuild and finish.** Drywall, texture, and paint are matched to the existing ceiling so the repaired section blends in rather than standing out as a patch.

## What separates a good ceiling repair response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating a ceiling stain as a paint problem instead of a moisture problem. A quick repaint over an unaddressed leak traps moisture behind a fresh coat, and mold can begin colonizing damp paper-faced drywall within 24 to 48 hours in Las Vegas's indoor climate-controlled homes, especially where insulation holds moisture against the back of the ceiling.

A second mistake is cutting out only the visibly stained area. Water spreads along the path of least resistance, often following a joist well past where the stain is visible, so an undersized cutout means hidden wet material gets sealed back up behind new drywall. Adjusters reviewing a ceiling claim typically want to see moisture readings documented before and after drying, photos of the removed material, and confirmation that the leak source was fixed, not just the cosmetic damage. Skipping that documentation is a common reason claims get delayed or underpaid.

## What does ceiling water damage repair cost?

Costs vary with how much of the ceiling is affected, whether insulation and framing need replacement, and whether texture and paint matching require a larger section to be redone for a seamless finish. Every loss is different, which is why a written scope is provided before any work begins so there are no surprises once drywall starts coming down.

| Scenario | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Small stain, drywall intact, repaint only | $250 - $600 |
| Single drywall section removed and replaced | $600 - $1,800 |
| Sagging section with wet insulation, partial ceiling rebuild | $1,800 - $4,500 |
| Multi-room ceiling damage from roof or plumbing failure | $4,500 - $10,000+ |
| Structural drying with air movers and dehumidifiers (per affected room) | $500 - $1,500 |

Homeowners insurance typically covers sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst supply line or a failed water heater, but it generally excludes damage from long-term neglected leaks or deferred roof maintenance, so the cause of the leak matters as much as the repair itself.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Las Vegas homes don't deal with freeze-thaw pipe bursts the way colder climates do, but the valley's summer monsoon season brings sudden, heavy rainfall that overwhelms aging or poorly sealed roof flashing, and flat or low-slope roofs common on older ranch-style homes in the area are especially prone to ponding and slow leaks after a storm. Many homes also run HVAC systems nearly year-round, and a clogged or disconnected condensate line above a ceiling can produce the same staining and sagging as a roof leak, just at a slower pace.
