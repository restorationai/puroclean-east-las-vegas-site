**Flooding inside your home or business?** Call now for emergency service before standing water spreads under flooring, into wall cavities, and toward electrical systems.

Flood damage is different from a burst pipe or an overflowing appliance. Floodwater usually arrives from outside the structure, storm runoff pushing under a slab, a monsoon downpour overwhelming drainage, a dry wash backing up into a yard, and it almost always carries silt, debris, and contamination that clean-water losses don't. That changes how the loss has to be handled from the first hour, including what can be saved and what has to come out.

## What flood damage restoration actually involves

Flood restoration starts with identifying the water category. Water that entered from outside, through soil, storm drains, or a wash, is treated as Category 3 (black water) because it can carry bacteria, chemicals, and sediment regardless of how clean it looks. That classification drives everything downstream: which materials are salvageable, what level of personal protective equipment the crew wears, and what has to be documented for the insurance claim before demolition starts. Work typically spans removing standing water, pulling out porous materials that absorbed contaminated water (carpet pad, baseboards, drywall below the flood line), disinfecting the structure, and running industrial drying equipment until moisture readings in the studs and subfloor return to normal. On a single affected room this can run a few days; a flooded basement or ground floor with saturated framing often takes longer because the structure itself has to dry, not just the surface.

## Our process

1. **Safety and contamination assessment.** Before anything else, the crew checks for electrical hazards, structural concerns, and confirms the water category so the right containment and PPE are used.
2. **Water extraction.** Submersible pumps and truck-mounted extractors remove standing water, prioritizing areas where water is pooling against framing or trapped under flooring.
3. **Contaminated material removal.** Carpet, pad, insulation, and drywall that absorbed flood water are removed to the height the water reached, since these materials typically can't be disinfected and dried in place.
4. **Cleaning and antimicrobial application.** Remaining structural surfaces are cleaned and treated to address the bacteria and contaminants flood water carries, not just the moisture itself.
5. **Drying and moisture monitoring.** Air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture meters confirm the subfloor, framing, and remaining drywall have returned to a dry standard, with readings logged for the claim file.

## What separates a good flood damage response from a bad one

The most common mistake on flood losses is treating them like a clean-water leak: drying in place instead of removing contaminated materials. Flood water that's allowed to dry inside a wall cavity or under flooring without proper removal and disinfection creates conditions for bacterial growth and odor that surface cleaning won't fix. A thorough response also documents the water line and affected materials with photos before demolition, since adjusters rely on that record to verify the scope. Another detail that gets missed: checking HVAC ducting and crawlspaces that flood water can reach even when the visible damage looks confined to one room. Moisture that's left behind in those hidden spaces is usually what turns into a mold claim weeks later.

## What does flood damage restoration cost?

Costs vary with how much water came in, how long it sat before cleanup started, and how many materials have to be removed versus dried in place. Homeowners insurance often excludes flood damage from a standard policy and requires separate flood coverage, though water damage from an internal source is usually handled differently, so it's worth confirming with your carrier what your policy actually covers before assuming it's included.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Las Vegas doesn't flood often, but when summer monsoon storms hit, they hit fast. The valley's hard-packed desert soil doesn't absorb water quickly, so a heavy downpour can push more runoff toward homes and low-lying streets than the storm drains can handle, especially in neighborhoods near washes and detention basins. Flash flood events tend to be short but intense, which means water can enter a structure quickly and sit before anyone realizes the extent of it. Acting fast once water is noticed matters more here than in climates with slower, longer-duration flooding.
