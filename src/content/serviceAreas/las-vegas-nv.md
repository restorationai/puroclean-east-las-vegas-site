## Restoration emergencies common in Las Vegas

Las Vegas weather swings hard between extremes, and both ends cause property damage. Summer monsoon storms dump intense rain in short bursts that overwhelm aging storm drains, especially around Downtown and older neighborhoods near Rancho Charleston, leading to flash flooding and water intrusion through foundations and window wells. The rest of the year, bone-dry heat stresses pipe fittings and slab plumbing, and a surge in water heater or supply line failures is common in homes built before the 1990s. Add in the valley's hard, mineral-heavy water, which accelerates corrosion in older galvanized and copper lines, and you get a steady mix of flash-flood calls and burst-pipe calls depending on the season.

## Services we provide in Las Vegas

Water damage in Las Vegas often involves either flash-flood intrusion through doors and foundations during monsoon season or a failed supply line inside a slab-on-grade home, both of which can hide moisture under flooring and inside wall cavities long after the surface looks dry. ## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Las Vegas

Much of Las Vegas was built from the 1950s through the 2000s on slab-on-grade foundations, with stucco exteriors and either built-up or tile roofing depending on the era. That slab construction means water from a burst pipe or monsoon flooding has nowhere to drain and tends to wick into drywall, baseboards, and cabinetry rather than pooling in a basement. Homes from the 1960s through 1980s may still carry galvanized supply lines or, in some cases, materials like asbestos-containing floor tile or texture that a restoration scope has to account for before demolition begins. The valley sits on caliche-heavy soil in many areas, a hard, cemented layer that drains poorly and can hold water against a foundation longer than typical desert sand would suggest. Structural repairs generally require permitting through the local building department, and many communities, particularly in Summerlin and Centennial Hills, add HOA approval requirements on top of that for exterior or structural work.
