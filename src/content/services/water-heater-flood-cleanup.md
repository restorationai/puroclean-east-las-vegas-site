## What water heater flood cleanup actually involves

Most Las Vegas water heaters sit in a garage corner, a hallway closet, or an attic platform, which means a burst tank or a slow leak at the drain valve often goes unnoticed until carpet is soaked or a ceiling below has stained. The cleanup starts with stopping the source: the water supply valve gets shut, and if the tank itself has failed structurally, it needs to come out before drying equipment goes in. From there the work is standard Category 1 water extraction, assuming the leak is fresh potable water and not sediment-laden tank sludge that has been sitting for days, which shifts classification and handling.

Equipment includes submersible pumps or wet vacuums for standing water, moisture meters to map how far the water traveled under baseboards and behind drywall, and air movers plus dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage. A contained garage leak might dry in two to three days. Water that reached an adjoining bedroom through a shared wall, or that pooled under vinyl plank flooring, usually runs longer because subfloor materials hold moisture that doesn't show on the surface.

## Our process

1. **Source shutoff and safety check.** The water supply line is closed and the area is checked for electrical hazards before anyone steps into standing water, since many Las Vegas water heaters are electric and share a utility closet with outlets or panels.
2. **Extraction.** Standing water is pulled with pumps and extraction wands, prioritizing areas where water has migrated toward bedrooms, hallways, or finished living space.
3. **Moisture mapping.** Meters and sometimes thermal imaging identify how far water traveled under flooring, behind baseboards, and into adjacent wall cavities, since a water heater closet rarely contains the full extent of the loss.
4. **Structural drying.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are set based on the moisture map, with daily monitoring to confirm drywall, subfloor, and framing are returning to normal moisture content rather than just surface-dry.
5. **Tank and area reconstruction notes.** If the water heater itself needs replacement or the closet drywall was compromised, that scope is documented separately so it can be handled by the right trade or folded into the restoration estimate.

## What separates a good water heater flood response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating the water heater closet as the whole job. Water from a burst tank doesn't stay in one room; it runs along the slab, under door thresholds, and into whatever flooring sits downhill from the source. A rushed response dries the visible puddle and calls it done, leaving moisture trapped under vinyl plank or tile that surfaces as a musty smell or soft subfloor weeks later.

A second issue is sediment. Las Vegas tap water is hard, and tanks that fail after years of mineral buildup can release water carrying rust and sediment that behaves differently than clean supply-line water. Treating that runoff like a simple clean-water leak, without adjusting cleaning agents or disposal of contaminated materials, is a shortcut that shows up later as staining or odor.

Insurance adjusters reviewing a water heater claim typically want documented moisture readings at the start and end of drying, photos of the failed unit or fitting, and a clear line between the plumbing failure (often not covered) and the resulting water damage (often covered). A written drying log makes that distinction easy to support.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Las Vegas homes run on slab foundations, which means a water heater leak rarely has a basement to drain into; it spreads laterally across the slab and under interior walls instead. Hard water across the valley accelerates sediment buildup inside tanks, which shortens their working life and raises the odds of a fitting or tank failure well before a homeowner expects one. Summer heat also means garages where many units sit can swing 100 plus degrees, which stresses tank seals and connections year-round rather than in just one season.
