## What storm damage restoration actually involves

Storm damage restoration covers the full chain of events after severe weather breaches a structure: securing the building so wind and rain can't cause further intrusion, removing standing water or wind-driven moisture, stabilizing or removing storm-damaged trees and debris, and drying out framing, drywall, and insulation that got wet during the event. In Las Vegas this most often means flat or low-slope roof damage from monsoon downpours, wind-torn roofing tile or shingles, stucco cracked by hail or debris impact, and flash flood water entering through grade-level doors, windows, or garages in low-lying neighborhoods. Equipment on a typical job includes tarping and plywood for emergency board-up, truck-mount extraction units for standing water, air movers and dehumidifiers for structural drying, and moisture meters to confirm hidden cavities have actually dried, not just the visible surface.

## Our process

1. **Emergency tarping and board-up.** Roof penetrations, broken windows, and torn siding get covered first to stop ongoing water and wind intrusion before anything else starts.
2. **Water extraction and debris clearing.** Standing water from flash flooding or roof failure is pulled out, and storm debris, downed branches, and damaged materials are cleared from the work area.
3. **Moisture mapping.** Walls, ceilings, and flooring near the breach point are checked with moisture meters to find where wind-driven rain traveled, since it rarely stays confined to the obvious entry point.
4. **Structural drying.** Air movers, dehumidifiers, and in some cases cavity drying systems are set to bring framing and insulation back to a normal moisture range, with daily monitoring until readings confirm the structure is dry.
5. **Documentation for repairs and claims.** Photos, moisture logs, and a written scope are compiled to support the insurance claim and to hand off to the trades handling roof or structural repair.

## What separates a good storm damage response from a bad one

The biggest mistake after a storm is treating it as a cleanup job instead of a moisture-tracking job. Wind-driven rain doesn't fall straight down, it gets pushed sideways into wall assemblies, around window flashing, and under roof underlayment, so a crew that only dries the visible wet spot often leaves saturated insulation or sheathing behind the wall. A thorough response checks adjacent rooms and the opposite side of exterior walls, not just the ceiling stain itself. Adjusters also look closely at the timeline between the storm event and when mitigation started, since delayed tarping or drying can be used to argue secondary damage was preventable. Good documentation, dated photos of the breach, moisture readings before and after drying, and a clear scope of work, protects the homeowner's claim and gives the adjuster what they need to approve repairs without back-and-forth.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Las Vegas monsoon season runs roughly July through September, bringing short, intense thunderstorms capable of dropping more rain in an hour than the desert soil and drainage washes can absorb. That combination of hard-packed clay soil and flat terrain means flash flooding can reach homes and garages quickly, even in neighborhoods that feel far from a wash. High-wind events, including dust storms that move through the valley, also loosen roofing material and stucco that then fails during the next heavy rain, so wind damage from one storm often sets up water damage from the next. Winter brings occasional strong wind events as well, which can affect loosely secured roofing on older homes throughout the valley.
