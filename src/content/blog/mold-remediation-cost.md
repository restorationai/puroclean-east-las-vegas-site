**TL;DR:** Mold remediation in Las Vegas typically costs $500 to $6,000, with most homeowners paying $1,500 to $3,500 for a contained, single-area job tied to a swamp-cooler leak or an HVAC condensation line. Price scales with square footage, whether the mold is surface-level or hidden inside a wall cavity, and whether sealed containment is needed in an occupied home. Whole-house jobs with mold behind drywall can run $10,000 or more.

If your swamp cooler just got drained and shut down for the season and you're seeing a dark ring spreading across a ceiling where the water line ran, or a musty smell showed up in a guest room after this summer's monsoon storms pushed water against the foundation, you're probably trying to figure out the bill before you call anyone. Pricing in the Las Vegas valley depends less on which company shows up and more on three things: how many square feet are affected, whether the mold is sitting on drywall paper or hiding behind it, and whether the home needs to stay occupied during the work. Here's what that actually costs, scenario by scenario.

## How much does mold remediation cost in Las Vegas?

Most Las Vegas homeowners pay between $1,500 and $4,000 for a single-area remediation job, and under $1,000 for small, surface-level mold caught early. The table below reflects typical ranges for the scenarios our crews see most often in the valley.

## What drives the cost up or down?

The biggest cost driver is how much material has to come out, not how large the room looks. A patch of surface mold on a bathroom ceiling tile costs far less to remove than the same square footage once it has traveled behind drywall or into wall insulation.

Other factors that move the number:

- **Source of moisture.** A one-time swamp-cooler overflow is a simpler fix than an ongoing slab leak that keeps re-wetting the area.
- **Accessibility.** Attic and crawlspace mold costs more per square foot because of tight working conditions and the extra time needed to rig containment overhead.
- **Occupied vs. vacant.** Working around furniture, pets, and daily life requires sealed poly containment and negative-air setup, which adds labor.
- **Testing and clearance.** Pre-remediation air or surface sampling and a post-job clearance test add cost but give you documented proof the air is clean again.
- **HVAC involvement.** If spores have circulated through the ductwork, cleaning or sealing the system adds a separate line item.

## Is mold remediation covered by homeowners insurance?

Mold remediation is usually covered only when the mold resulted from a sudden, covered event, like a burst supply line, not from gradual leaks or humidity the homeowner could have caught sooner. Most Nevada policies carry a separate mold sub-limit, often $1,000 to $10,000, even when the underlying water damage itself is covered in full.

That makes documentation the whole game. Adjusters want photos of the source, moisture readings showing when and where water entered, and a clear timeline. If a pipe failed overnight and mold shows up within 48-72 hours, that's a very different claim than mold discovered after months of an unreported swamp-cooler drip. Our [water damage insurance guide](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) walks through how Nevada adjusters typically treat the underlying water loss, which is often the bigger half of the claim.

If you're not sure whether what you're looking at is actually mold yet, it's worth reading [how to test for mold](/blog/how-to-test-for-mold/) before you call your insurer, since a documented lab result strengthens the claim either way.

## What does the mold remediation process actually involve?

A standard job follows the same sequence the [EPA's mold cleanup guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home) describes, regardless of who performs it:

1. **Inspection and moisture mapping.** A technician identifies the mold's extent and the moisture source using a moisture meter or thermal camera.
2. **Containment setup.** Poly sheeting seals off the work area and a negative-air machine keeps spores from spreading to the rest of the home.
3. **HEPA air filtration.** An air scrubber runs continuously during demo to capture airborne spores.
4. **Removal of contaminated material.** Affected drywall, insulation, or flooring is bagged and removed; non-porous surfaces are cleaned and treated instead of replaced.
5. **Drying and moisture correction.** The root cause gets fixed, whether that's a swamp-cooler pad, a slab leak, or an HVAC condensation line, so the mold doesn't come back.
6. **Clearance testing.** A third-party or in-house air sample confirms spore counts are back to a normal background level before containment comes down.
